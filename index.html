Finance Ops

30 / 60 / 90 for a two-person finance team

Protect cash, build controls that don’t need headcount, and automate the repetitive work so the team scales without hiring.

Start with days 1–30 See the three jobs

Operating model

Three jobs. Two people. No spare capacity.

Built for a small firm with project-based revenue—T&M, fixed-fee, and milestone billing—plus meaningful AI and tooling costs.

  1. 01

    Protect cash

    Know runway weekly. Shorten order-to-cash. Chase what’s already owed before building anything new.

  2. 02

    Lean controls

    Segregate revenue vs. cost ownership. Approval thresholds and dual sign-off that two people can actually run.

  3. 03

    Automate to scale

    Invoice, reminders, expenses, recon—remove hours per invoice so headcount stays flat as volume grows.

Days 1–30

Stabilize and see clearly

Goal: know where the money is and make sure nothing breaks.

Cash visibility

Build a 13-week rolling cash forecast. This is the single most important artifact for a small firm. See a sample 13-week model →

Baseline order-to-cash

Map how work becomes an invoice and then cash. Measure DSO, the unbilled WIP backlog, and overdue AR by client.

Split the work

One person owns revenue (billing, collections, revenue recognition). The other owns cost (payables, payroll, contractors, expenses, tooling). Basic segregation of duties with only two people.

Quick wins

Chase overdue invoices, fix billing delays, and make sure contracts have clear payment terms.

Cost audit

List all AI and software spend (LLM APIs, licenses, cloud) and tag it to clients or projects where possible.

Month-end close

Document the current process and set a target—close in 5 business days.

First-30-days checklist

Mark what you finish. Progress stays on this device.

Days 31–60

Build the engine

Goal: automate the repeatable work and get project-level profitability.

Automate finance workflows

  • Invoice generation from timesheets or milestones
  • Automated payment reminders and collections nudges
  • Expense and receipt capture and coding
  • Bank reconciliation and AP matching
  • AI-assisted anomaly detection on invoices, expenses, and contractor payments

Project profitability

Track margin per engagement, including labor, contractors, and AI/tooling. AI-native firms often under-count token and compute costs.

Revenue recognition

Define how fixed-fee, milestone, and retainer work is recognized, and set up the monthly process.

Controls

Approval thresholds, dual approval for payments above a set limit, and vendor onboarding checks—keep them lightweight.

Reporting

One monthly dashboard: revenue, gross margin by project, utilization, DSO, cash runway, and forecast vs. actual.

Days 61–90

Optimize and become a strategic partner

Goal: shift from record-keeping to decision support.

Pricing input

Work with leadership on pricing for AI-delivered work. If AI cuts delivery hours, hourly billing erodes revenue—push toward fixed-fee, outcome-based, or value-based pricing using your margin data.

Forecasting

Add a pipeline-to-revenue forecast and scenario planning: losing the top client, adding a hire, an LLM price change.

Concentration & credit risk

Flag client exposure and set payment-term policies before one account can break the cash plan.

Scale readiness

Decide what stays automated, what needs a part-time bookkeeper or fractional controller, and which tools to consolidate.

Compliance

Review tax (sales tax, R&D credits, contractor classification), audit readiness, and data security around financial and client data.

Retrospective

Review automations for accuracy and time saved, then set the next-year finance roadmap.

Guardrails

Key risks to manage

  • Key-person dependency

    Document everything so either person can cover for the other.

  • Over-automating too early

    Fix the process before you automate it.

  • Hidden AI costs

    Usage-based spend can spike—set budgets and alerts on LLM and cloud usage.
